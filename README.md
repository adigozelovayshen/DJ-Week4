📈Marketing Data Analysis - Week 4
This repository contains the analysis of marketing performance metrics, SQL query implementations for data extraction, and strategic recommendations based on the provided dataset.

🔗TASK 1: Marketing Metrics Definitions
The following key performance indicators (KPIs) were selected to evaluate marketing efficiency:

Revenue: SUM(revenue). Represents the total monetary value generated by marketing activities.

ROAS (Return on Ad Spend): revenue / spend. Measures the revenue generated per 1 AZN spent on advertising.

CPA (Cost Per Acquisition): spend / conversions. Indicates the cost incurred to acquire a single customer or conversion.

Conversion Rate: (conversions / sessions) * 100. Measures the percentage of website visitors who complete a conversion.

Spend: SUM(spend). The total budget utilized for marketing campaigns.

AOV (Average Order Value): revenue / conversions. The average monetary value per transaction.

🔗TASK 2: SQL Data Extraction and Analysis
The analysis utilizes SQL to perform comparative studies and trend monitoring.

Period-over-Period Comparison: Segments data into two halves (2024 vs. 2025) to calculate total sessions, conversions, conversion rates, spend, revenue, and ROAS.

Regional and Channel Performance: Groups data by region, channel, and year to identify specific performance drivers.

Campaign Objective Analysis: Joins the events and campaigns tables to track monthly ROAS trends by campaign objective.

Budget and Revenue Share: Uses subqueries to determine the percentage share of spend and revenue for each channel in 2025.

CPA Trend Analysis (Simpson's Paradox): Compares 2024 and 2025 CPA and conversion metrics side-by-side to detect shifting effects within channels.

Monthly Seasonal Trends: Analyzes monthly revenue share and CPA to isolate seasonal performance fluctuations.

🔗TASK 3: Strategic Insights
The analysis yielded three critical insights:

Efficiency Trends: While revenue grew from 5.9M to 6.6M AZN, the CPA increased from 3.8 to 4.0 AZN, indicating higher acquisition costs.

Simpson's Paradox: Individual channel performance metrics (e.g., Paid Search) remained stable or improved; however, the overall average CPA rose due to a "mix-shift" effect, where the budget was increasingly allocated to more expensive, lower-conversion channels.

Seasonality: Observed revenue spikes in November/December are primarily due to seasonal events (e.g., Black Friday, New Year) rather than purely marketing-driven success. Retention and CRM activities were identified as the primary drivers of high-efficiency performance during these periods.

🔗TASK 4: Data Visualization
Aggregate CPA Trend: Confirms the rise in total CPA from 3.86 to 3.98 AZN across the observed periods.

Channel-Level CPA Comparison: Demonstrates that while aggregate CPA increased, individual channel efficiency remained stable, proving the shift in budget allocation as the root cause.

Monthly Revenue Dynamics: Highlights seasonal peaks, suggesting that monthly comparisons must account for market-wide behavioral patterns.

🔗TASK 5: Marketing Performance Strategy
Comprehensive Monitoring: Do not rely on "Grand Total" metrics alone; individual channel dynamics must be monitored to avoid misleading conclusions.

Budget Optimization: Adjust the budget mix by reducing reliance on high-CPA channels and reallocating resources to high-efficiency channels (e.g., Email, Organic Search).

Contextual Evaluation: Always factor in seasonality when comparing performance across different months to avoid misinterpreting organic growth as campaign efficacy.

🔗TASK 6: Recommendations
Based on the analysis of the "mix-shift" effect, the following strategy is proposed:

Budget Reallocation: Conduct a 1-month trial by reallocating 10% of the Paid Search budget (CPA ~11.15 AZN) to the Email channel (CPA ~0.44 AZN).

Performance Evaluation: Post-trial, evaluate whether the total revenue remains stable while the aggregate CPA decreases. If successful, formalize the budget reallocation to improve long-term marketing profitability.
